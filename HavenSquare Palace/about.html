HavenSquare Palace: A Storied Oasis Near Times Square

Nestled just a block away from the electric buzz of New York’s Times Square, HavenSquare Palace stands as a beacon of luxury and history—a hotel that has witnessed the evolution of Manhattan from the Gilded Age to the neon-lit present.

The Gilded Beginnings (1890s-1920s)

HavenSquare Palace first opened its doors in 1898, during the height of New York’s Gilded Age. Designed by architect Stanford White (of the famed firm McKim, Mead & White), the hotel was originally envisioned as an exclusive retreat for wealthy industrialists and Broadway elites. Its Beaux-Arts façade, adorned with intricate carvings and grand archways, quickly made it a landmark.

In its early years, HavenSquare Palace hosted opera singers, tycoons like the Vanderbilts, and even European royalty passing through New York. Rumor has it that Enrico Caruso, the legendary tenor, once stayed in the penthouse suite after a performance at the Metropolitan Opera.

The Roaring Twenties & Prohibition Secrets (1920s-1930s)

During Prohibition, HavenSquare Palace became a speakeasy hotspot. A hidden basement lounge—accessed through a fake bookshelf in the lobby—served bootlegged champagne to high-society rebels, including F. Scott Fitzgerald and Josephine Baker. The hotel’s staff famously kept silent, even during police raids.

Hollywood Glamour & Decline (1940s-1970s)

Post-World War II, HavenSquare Palace became a favorite of Hollywood stars filming in New York. Marilyn Monroe allegedly stayed here during the filming of The Seven Year Itch (1955), and Frank Sinatra hosted after-parties in the ballroom.

However, by the 1970s, as Times Square fell into disrepair, the hotel struggled. It narrowly escaped demolition, saved only by its historic designation in 1976.

Revival & Modern Luxury (1980s-Present)

After a multi-million-dollar restoration in the late 1980s, HavenSquare Palace re-emerged as a five-star boutique hotel, blending its historic charm with modern luxury. The meticulous renovation ensured that while modern amenities were integrated, the grandeur and intricate details of its past, from the Beaux-Arts façade to the original 1920s chandeliers in the Crystal Lounge, were lovingly preserved.

The name "HavenSquare Palace" itself was thoughtfully chosen to reflect this unique blend of heritage, prime location, and inherent grandeur. "Haven" evokes the desired sense of homey comfort and an exclusive, serene retreat amidst the city's hustle. Meanwhile, "Square" unequivocally signifies its iconic and central position within the vibrant New York City area, just steps away from Times Square. The inclusion of "Palace" underscores its historical opulence, its architectural magnificence, and its status as a luxurious, grand establishment that has hosted royalty and celebrities since the Gilded Age, truly making it an esteemed oasis.

Today, guests can experience this harmonious blend, enjoying artisanal cocktails in a historic setting or taking in stunning views of the Times Square skyline from the rooftop bar.
